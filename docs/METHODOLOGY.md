# Garin–Lowry methodology

This is a deterministic theoretical spatial simulation of a hypothetical city, not a population forecast. The methodology follows the supplied Liu and Wang lab manual, Case 10, printed pp. 196–203, DOI [10.1201/9781003304357-10](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781003304357-10). The supplied workflow is the numerical reference. The archived description is available on [NodePit](https://nodepit.com/workflow/com.knime.hub/Users/nabilarpa/Public/Case10-Implementing%20the%20Garin-Lowry%20Model%20in%20a%20Hypothetical%20City). No unverified Wang (1998) bibliographic details are asserted.

## Quantities and orientation

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| B | Exogenous basic employment column vector | Model jobs |
| P | Residential population column vector | Model persons |
| S | Endogenous service employment column vector | Model jobs |
| E = B + S | Total employment | Model jobs |
| h = 2 | Population supported per total worker | Persons/job |
| e = 0.3 | Service workers generated per resident | Jobs/person |
| a, b | Residential and service friction exponents | Dimensionless |
| D, R | Shortest-path distance and duration between projected centroid locations | Projected m, minutes |
| c | Amended model travel impedance | Model minutes |

Code uses `population_per_worker`, `service_per_person`, `residential_exponent` and `service_exponent` to avoid confusing demographic multipliers with friction coefficients, which the manual calls alpha and beta. Rows are receiving zones; columns are zones producing the quantity being allocated. Zone vectors and both matrix axes are ordered by unique `ORIG_FID` 1–136.

## Network and local costs

For road edge k, length is measured from the supplied EPSG:3857 polyline and time is:

`t_k = length_k / (speed_k × 26.8224)`.

One mph equals 26.8224 metres/minute. The graph is undirected because no directional restrictions were supplied. Centroids are projected onto their nearest polyline and the polyline is split at each projected location; Dijkstra routing uses these inserted vertices. Distances are never replaced with straight-line interzonal distances. Network diagonal entries are zero. The same node-rounding and small topology-gap assumptions are recorded in network diagnostics; this dataset needs no artificial connection.

Original Math Formula #7 is preserved:

`c_ij = R_ij + (PERIMETER_i + PERIMETER_j) × 0.25 × 1000 / 804.67`.

Each quarter-perimeter contributes a local access impedance at the baseline 30 mph. This produces a positive intrazonal cost. For example, perimeters of 4 and 6 legacy kilometres add `10 × 250 / 804.67 ≈ 3.107` model minutes to the network duration. The exact denominator 804.67 is retained even though the more precise speed conversion gives 804.672. Measured snap offsets are exported but not added again, avoiding a different baseline definition.

The geometry and historical length attributes have a slight scaling mismatch. The road routing uses actual projected metre coordinates while local access follows the documented legacy perimeter convention. Both must be reported as assumptions of this instructional model.

## Gravity allocation

For receiving zone i and producing zone j, define:

`F_ij = c_ij^(-a)` and `H_ij = c_ij^(-b)`.

Then:

`G_ij = h × F_ij / sum_k F_kj`

`T_ij = e × H_ij / sum_k H_kj`.

Each column of G sums to h: all residents supported by a producing job are allocated across receiving residential zones. Each column of T sums to e: service employment generated by a residential zone is allocated across receiving service zones. No attraction weights or land-capacity constraints were supplied, so none are introduced.

For a simple two-zone example, if both costs from a producing job are equal, G allocates one person to each zone, totalling two. A resident facing equal service allocation costs generates 0.15 service jobs in each zone, totalling 0.3. Larger exponents penalize more distant receiving locations relative to nearer ones.

## Feedback and equilibrium

Employment creates residential demand: `P = G E`. Residents generate services: `S = T P`. With `E = B + S`:

`P = G(B + TP)`

`(I − GT)P = GB`.

The independent core solves this linear system using `scipy.linalg.solve`, without explicitly forming an inverse. Service employment is then `S = TP`, and total employment is `E = B + S`.

An independent fixed-point calculation starts with `P^(0)=0` and repeats:

`P^(k+1) = G(B + TP^(k))`.

It stops when the maximum absolute update is at most `1e-11` model persons, with a maximum of 1000 iterations. Finite/nonnegative inputs, residuals, method agreement and convergence are checked. For these nonnegative column-normalized matrices, `h e < 1` guarantees a stable nonnegative equilibrium; the baseline feedback spectral radius is 0.6. The baseline converges in 53 iterations.

Summing across zones gives:

`P_total = h B_total / (1 − he)` and `S_total = e P_total`.

At B_total=100, h=2 and e=0.3, the exact theoretical totals are 500 residents and 150 service jobs. The source stores B_total=99.999984, yielding 499.999920 and 149.999976. These are fractional model quantities, not an observed headcount or a rounding defect to conceal.

## Why the original transpose is retained

Native nodes #11 and #16 produce column-normalized service and residential matrices. Nodes #12 and #17 transpose them. Original #18 computes `I − G.T @ T.T`; original #21 solves its transpose. This gives `I − T @ G`. With the supplied baseline and experiments, a=b and G is proportional to T; their products commute, so this equals the required `I − G @ T` exactly to numerical precision. Original output agrees with the conservation and balance equations. The native scripts are therefore preserved.

For **unequal** residential and service friction exponents, products need not commute. The extended core explicitly forms the correct `G @ T`, and a three-zone test with unequal exponents checks this orientation. Do not change just one original Math Formula friction exponent and assume the legacy transpose chain remains valid.

## Experiments and interpretation

The saved uniform baseline is followed by CBD employment concentration, equal friction exponents 0.5 and 2, and a ring-7 speed increase from 30 to 75 mph. Two additional CBD cases reproduce the manual's comparative high-friction and beltway designs. Every scenario conserves the saved basic-employment total and uses identical zone IDs. The faster network is copied and rerouted; it is not a manually invented cost matrix.

Maps show model quantities per zone. Polygons have different areas, so a choropleth of counts is not a density map. Scenario population panels share one common colour range, and population-change panels share a symmetric range; the separate beltway map explicitly uses its own smaller scale. Concentration is summarized by CBD population share, population HHI and population-weighted projected distance to the CBD. The ten original `TRACT0_ID` records form a transect; all-zone analysis uses `zone_id`.
